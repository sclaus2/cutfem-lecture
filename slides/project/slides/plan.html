Why cut?

Meshing a complex or moving geometry is the bottleneck: we mesh a simple box and cut the geometry out of it with a level set. Three examples show how.

One minute. The map of the lecture: three examples, and one long one first. Why cut and the three examples (8 minutes). Then Example 1, Poisson on a flower, built in six steps, each explained and then written with minicut: level set and classification, quadrature, boundary conditions with Nitsche, the conditioning problem with its two remedies (ghost penalty and aggregation), and solve and convergence. Then the same steps run on an image of a porous specimen (a scan turned into a level set, solved in the solid matrix) and on a rotor built from level sets, turned through a hot jet and tuned in an optimisation loop. The lecture closes with how to try it yourself, the key ideas and questions. Say that this lecture builds on the week: weak form and Galerkin (lecture 1), meshes, P1 and assembly (lecture 2), convergence and cost (lecture 3).
