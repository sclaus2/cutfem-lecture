Lagrange multipliers: the derivation

1 Constrained minimisation

Minimise the energy among functions with u = g on Γ. The multiplier λ is a new function on Γ.

2 Stationary point

Vary u: the weak form with an extra boundary term. Vary λ: the constraint, weakly.

3 What is λ?

Integrating by parts shows λ = −∂u/∂n on Γ: the flux through the boundary, often what we want to know.

Four minutes. First of the two classical alternatives to Nitsche. Minimise the energy with the constraint u = g on Gamma, and add a Lagrange multiplier lambda that lives on Gamma only. Taking the variation in u gives the usual weak form plus the term integral of lambda times v over Gamma; taking it in lambda gives the constraint in weak form. Integrating by parts shows that lambda is minus the normal derivative: the flux. This is physically meaningful, for instance the heat flux through the wall. The price is paid in the next slide. Sign convention: the Lagrangian here is energy minus work plus lambda times (u minus g). In minicut this is mc.lagrange_system (notebook section 5b): the multiplier is a function on Gamma, and the plot there compares it with the exact flux minus the normal derivative of u, which is the check of step 3.
